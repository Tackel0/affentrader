# Recovering interrupted native candle history

Requested native Hyperliquid series now recover available candles after a stream gap. The Vela feed's from-only range request performs a fresh provider fetch instead of returning the existing cache. Recovery starts at the previously observed candle so its stale close and volume can also be repaired.

The service keeps the latest native snapshots while a request is in flight, then merges their cumulative volume with provider history. Parallel requests for the same series share one operation. Technical history is unavailable through `barsFor` until the gap is repaired. Tick-based price observations remain separate. Historical close events are not sent to price/technical alert or paper-execution listeners.

The chart feed first corrects the interrupted bucket, then publishes the newest bucket. Vela's native gap healer requests the complete repair batch through its range port and recalculates studies after the batch. A short-lived publication scope serves this already fetched repair without another HTTP call; ordinary from-only requests still fetch fresh data. This avoids replaying thousands of live bar notifications. Subscriber exceptions are isolated, and unsubscribed/stale-generation consumers are fenced.

Disconnects and public-feed refreshes also suspend technical history until a valid, non-stale full candle snapshot arrives. An unchanged fresh snapshot is sufficient for a same-bucket reconnect. A reconnect crossing a bucket boundary re-fetches the interrupted close before resuming, without replaying that close as a new signal. Responses from a closed/replaced socket or an earlier feed generation cannot publish their results.

Temporary errors retry with bounded backoff. Automatic repairs are started two at a time. An absent interruption anchor or an outage beyond provider retention is reported as unavailable, with a Reload chart action; missing buckets are not fabricated. The gap state is transient and is excluded from saved workspaces. Main and satellite charts show waiting/recovery/retry/unavailable states. The primary Replay view keeps its own historical readout and does not show the live-feed recovery badge.

The main menu now provides **Reconnect market data**. It preserves chart history, studies, drawings and the market generation while reopening public feeds. It is disabled in explicit Simulation. This is separate from connecting a broker or resetting the local paper account.

## Verification

- Twelve recovery-window/merge cases include minute and calendar-month boundaries, retention caps, invalid requests, preserving earlier/later data, absent anchors, bounded memory and refusing to pad empty venue buckets.
- The actual service tests cover fresh from-only requests, coalescing, newer in-flight native updates, retry after HTTP errors, stale socket/feed results, unavailable retention, reconnect freshness and resuming normal closes after repair. A 1,000-bucket interruption test verifies that the native range seam receives all 1,001 anchor-to-current buckets with only two repair notifications, that no extra range fetch is made, and that a failing renderer subscriber does not poison the recovery. This tests the feed protocol, not a browser timing benchmark for 1,000 candles. Existing native feed/parser tests remain part of the focused group.
- `scripts/verify-hyperliquid-gap.mjs` runs the real MarketService with public WebSocket and provider history data in isolated in-memory stores. It suppresses incoming deliveries only in that test client for 130 seconds, including across watchdog reconnections. It then verifies restored closed candles against actual source messages/HTTP responses, checks that incomplete technical history was withheld and rejects any historical close signal. No market price or candle is synthesized and no browser/account state is changed. The evidence is `artifacts/candle-recovery-live.json` and `.log`.
- Full-suite, build and standalone typecheck results use the `artifacts/candle-recovery-` prefix. Browser observations are recorded in the production checkpoint.

The final gate passed **1,057 tests across 86 files**, production build and standalone TypeScript verification. The focused recovery/parser/service group passed 64 tests. In the final live run, 357 incoming deliveries were suppressed over 130 seconds across three sockets. The missed closed BTC/1m candle was restored from actual provider data, technical history was withheld until repair, and historical close signals remained zero. Only the interrupted/latest boundary updates were published to chart subscribers; the complete repair stayed available through the native range port.

The production browser exercised Main menu → Reconnect market data with an already loaded chart. It displayed Waiting for fresh candles and Reconnecting to Hyperliquid, then fresh OHLCV and Market data connected with no remaining recovery badge. Captured browser warnings/errors were empty. EMA/Volume, BTC/5m/UTC, the five-symbol watchlist and the collapsed bottom panel remained. Existing alerts still showed 15 total, 0 active and 123 log entries. Screenshots: `candle-recovery-reconnect.png`, `candle-recovery-production.png`; state evidence: `candle-recovery-browser.json`. The transient-state capture must start after OHLC is actually loaded; a click immediately during initial boot can complete before any candle subscription exists.

This covers requested native Hyperliquid candle series and the venue history that is actually available. It does not restore missing individual trades/depth, prove every market/interval/device, repair provider corrections outside retained revisions, or establish the same recovery contract for Binance, Yahoo and custom/resampled intervals. A full 1:1 TradingView acceptance remains open.

The provider exposes only its most recent 5,000 candles, which bounds recovery. [Hyperliquid candleSnapshot documentation](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint#candle-snapshot).

The later [socket-ownership correction](recovery-socket-ownership.md) fences same-generation late repair failures and detaches old pending repairs when a replacement socket is installed. It includes failing-before/passing-after race tests and a new completed public outage probe.
